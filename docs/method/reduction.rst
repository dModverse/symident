Reduction
=========

A reduction replaces the coordinates by combinations that the output determines :cite:p:`Chappell1998,Evans2000,Massonis2023`, the invariants :math:`I_l(\theta)` with :math:`X(I_l) = 0`
:cite:p:`Olver1986`. The directions are reduced in *blocks*, sets of directions with overlapping
supports, each block with the group :math:`G` generated by the flows of the generators of the block.
symident searches, within degree bounds, monomial, polynomial and rational invariants, Darboux
polynomials and exponential factors :cite:p:`Prelle1983,Goriely2001`, i.e., polynomials :math:`P`
and exponentials :math:`e^{P/Q}` that :math:`X` multiplies by a polynomial, and invariants by
separable quadrature and by an integrating factor. The invariants become new parameters,
:math:`I_l(\theta) = q_l`, coordinates on the orbit space :math:`\Theta / G`, a *chart*, and the
trafo is the section in these coordinates. A chart is returned only where it is valid on the
declared domain, the positive orthant by default; ``Reduction.partial`` names the directions whose
chart covers the domain only in part, and ``alternatives=True`` also returns the charts of the other
valid choices of the fixed coordinates.

.. _red-scalings:

Scalings
--------

A scaling is removed by fixing one of the coordinates it moves at one, a *pin*. Every orbit of a
scaling meets the hypersurface :math:`\theta_i = 1` of a moved positive coordinate once, so the
result is a *global section*: a one-to-one map of the new parameters onto a *global
cross-section*, a set meeting every orbit exactly once. In the example of Equation
:eq:`eq-example`, :math:`A_0 = 1` meets every orbit of the scaling once. A direction along one
coordinate is pinned like a scaling where a *sign certificate*, a proof that an expression has one
sign on :math:`\Theta`, shows that the generator component on the pinned coordinate has one sign;
else the pin is listed under ``unproven``.

The pinned coordinates of a block of scalings, the *transversal*, are coordinates :math:`T` for
which the columns :math:`W_T` of the weight matrix :math:`W` are invertible and :math:`W_T^{-1} W`
has integer entries, so that every other coordinate becomes a monomial of the new parameters;
without one the block keeps the invariants only (``invariant_only``). A pin on a coordinate not
declared positive misses the orbits where that coordinate is not positive, so positive transversals
come first. Where these lie in the support of general directions, each general generator :math:`X`
becomes :math:`X - \sum_i \lambda_i S_i`, with the scalings :math:`S_i`, so that it leaves the pins
in place and spans the same distribution together with the scalings. A block of several general
generators is split by *module reduction* into combinations with smaller supports.

.. _red-linear:

Linear flows
------------

A block of :math:`r` general generators is a *linear flow* when, in the coordinates
:math:`\tilde\theta_i = \theta_i` for a moved coordinate that is not declared positive and
:math:`\tilde\theta_i = \log \theta_i` for a positive one, every component :math:`\tilde\xi_i`
depends only on the coordinates :math:`u` that the block does not move, and no declared bound
involves a moved coordinate. The generators then commute, and the orbit of a point is the affine
set :math:`\{(u, \tilde\theta + \Xi(u) s) : s \in \mathbb{R}^r\}`. Pins :math:`\tilde\theta_P = 0`
on :math:`r` moved coordinates whose minor :math:`\det \Xi_P(u)` has one sign on :math:`\Theta` give
a global section: :math:`\tilde\theta_P + \Xi_P(u) s = 0` has exactly one solution on every orbit,
so the section is ``injective`` and ``reach``-es every orbit. The invariants are
:math:`\tilde\theta_N - \Xi_N(u) \Xi_P(u)^{-1} \tilde\theta_P` for the other moved coordinates, and
a linear coordinate is pinned to 0, a positive one to 1.

.. _red-general:

General blocks
--------------

For a block of :math:`r` general generators the invariants :math:`I(\theta) = q` are solved for
coordinates :math:`\theta_s = R(q, \vartheta)`, with :math:`r` *gauge coordinates* :math:`\vartheta`
left. Every level set :math:`\Lambda_q = \{\theta \in \Theta : I(\theta) = q\}` is one orbit when

1. :math:`R(q, \vartheta)` is the unique solution for every gauge value, so :math:`\Lambda_q` is the
   graph of :math:`R(q, \cdot)` over :math:`\mathcal{G}_q = \{\vartheta : (R(q, \vartheta),
   \vartheta) \in \Theta\}`;
2. the :math:`r \times r` minor of the generator components on :math:`\vartheta` has one sign on
   :math:`\Theta`, so every orbit is :math:`r`-dimensional and open in :math:`\Lambda_q`;
3. :math:`\mathcal{G}_q` is connected.

:math:`\mathcal{G}_q` is given by one inequality :math:`\pi_k(q, \vartheta) > 0` per positive
solved coordinate. It is connected when every inequality is convex in :math:`\vartheta`, or when a
gauge coordinate enters every inequality affinely with a coefficient of known sign and the
projection is connected, decided the same way. The *section point* :math:`\hat\theta(q)` then lies
in :math:`\Lambda_q` and, by a *positivity certificate*, a proof that each entry is positive on the
domain, in :math:`\Theta`.

**Sections.** Sections equate *balances*, monomials or summands of the invariants. Where the
section point holds a root but the section itself is rational, a *section chart* takes the remaining
coordinates of the section as new parameters, which the count of discrete symmetries
(:meth:`Symmetries.discrete`) needs.

**Coverage.** ``reach`` says that every level set of the invariants is one orbit; with *total
coverage* every orbit then meets the section, under *partial coverage* exactly the orbits whose
invariants lie where the solved entries are positive. Under declared bounds an entry without
positivity certificate is kept, with partial coverage, and listed under ``unproven``. Without bounds
it is kept only where no section is certified on the whole domain, and the transformation then
covers the orbits on which the ``sign_conditions``, the entries as inequalities in :math:`q`, hold.

**Face sections.** A *face* is a boundary set :math:`\theta_f = 0`; a section on a face lies outside
:math:`\Theta` and is reached only in a limit. With :math:`\theta_f` as gauge coordinates, the
conditions above make every level set one orbit, and :math:`R(q, \theta_f) \to R(q, 0) > 0` puts the
face point in the closure of every orbit of :math:`\Lambda_q`. The output equals that at the face
point when the model is continuous there: no model expression has a pole at the face, and under
``equilibrate`` the resting state has a limit, shown by invariants of the generators lifted to the
resting variety, :math:`\xi_x = -J_x^{-1} J_\theta \xi_\theta`, proven where :math:`\det J_x` has
one sign on :math:`\Theta`, else checked at random face points (``face_level``).

**Injectivity.** That every orbit meets the cross-section :math:`\mathcal{C}` at most once
(``injective``) is proven when the solved coordinates recover every point of :math:`\mathcal{C}`
from the invariants, :math:`\hat\theta(I(\theta)) = \theta` on :math:`\mathcal{C}`, shown over
:math:`\mathbb{Q}` on a parametrization of the positive points of :math:`\mathcal{C}`. For one
generator it also holds when :math:`X` changes the pinned coordinate with one sign on
:math:`\Theta`.

**Zero limits.** With ``zero_limits=True`` each block reports which coordinates can be set to zero:
everywhere, under a condition, or in a limit along the orbits, and whether this is certain
(``to_dict()["zero_compatibility"]``).

An unproven claim is listed under ``unproven`` of the block and makes the block ``open``; a block
is ``verified`` or ``checked`` when a direction or a face resting state has that level, else
``exact``. The transformation is checked over :math:`\mathbb{Q}`, and ``check=True`` runs the
detection again on the reduced model (``Reduction.checks["loop"]``).

.. _red-inputs:

Unknown inputs
--------------

In a model with ``unknown_inputs`` :math:`w`, a direction changes the input trajectory together with
:math:`\theta` and the initial values. The reduction keeps the inputs free and proves, per
direction, that they absorb the change, in one of two ways; a direction with neither proof is not
reduced. For a model with one condition, no events and no ``equilibrate``, :math:`\dot x = f(x,
\theta, w, u)`, output :math:`h(x, \theta)`, with :math:`D = f \cdot \partial_x`:

*Inversion.* Suppose there are as many observables as unknown inputs, none reads an input, and
:math:`M = \partial_w D(h)` has a determinant that is not zero as a rational function. Let
:math:`(\hat\theta, \hat x_0, \hat w)` give the output :math:`\hat g` on :math:`[0, T]` with
:math:`\det M \ne 0` along the trajectory. Then every :math:`(\theta', x_0')` near
:math:`(\hat\theta, \hat x_0)` with :math:`h(x_0', \theta') = \hat g(0)` gives :math:`\hat g` with
some continuous input :math:`w'`.

*Proof.* The implicit function theorem solves :math:`D(h)(x, \theta, w) = \dot{\hat g}(t)` for
:math:`w = W(t, x, \theta)` near the trajectory, the solution of :math:`\dot x = f(x, \theta, W)`
exists on :math:`[0, T]` by continuous dependence, and :math:`e = h(x', \theta') - \hat g` has
:math:`\dot e = 0`, :math:`e(0) = 0`. :math:`\square`

Every exact direction keeps :math:`h(x_0, \theta)`, so a section chosen as without inputs gives the
output of every point under this condition.

*Pointwise lift.* Suppose the direction has components :math:`\xi_\theta(\theta)`,
:math:`\xi_x(\theta, x)` and :math:`\xi_w(\theta, x, w)` with

.. math::
   :label: eq-input-lift

   D(\xi_x) = V(f), \qquad V(h) = 0, \qquad V = \xi_\theta\, \partial_\theta + \xi_x\,
   \partial_x + \xi_w\, \partial_w ,

over :math:`\mathbb{Q}`, and a given initial value moves with the parameters. Then the flow of
:math:`V` applied to :math:`(\hat\theta, \hat x(t), \hat w(t))` gives, for small :math:`\varepsilon`,
a solution of the model with :math:`\theta(\varepsilon)` and the input :math:`w(\cdot, \varepsilon)`
and the output :math:`\hat g`.

*Proof.* With :math:`R = \partial_t x - f(x, \theta, w)`, Equation :eq:`eq-input-lift` gives
:math:`\partial_\varepsilon R = \partial_x \xi_x\, R` with :math:`R(t, 0) = 0`, hence :math:`R = 0`,
and :math:`\partial_\varepsilon h = V(h) = 0`. :math:`\square`

The reduction removes the field :math:`\xi_\theta` as long as the input stays in the domain of the
inputs; initial values and inputs stay free. ``Reduction.blocks`` holds per block ``inputs``: the
proof (``"inversion"`` with the ``determinant``, or ``"lift"`` with the components) and the
``condition`` on the input and the trajectory, on which the section claims rest.

.. _red-branches:

Branches of discrete symmetries
-------------------------------

For a reduction, each image of the fundamental domain under the maps is a *branch*. Where a map
:math:`\sigma` has one hypersurface :math:`q = 0` as fixed set and exchanges the two sides of it,
:math:`q(\sigma(\theta)) = -c\, q(\theta)` with :math:`c > 0` on :math:`\Theta`, the branches are
the sides :math:`s\, q > 0`, :math:`s = \pm 1`. On a side, :math:`\delta = s\, q(\theta) > 0`
replaces a coordinate :math:`x` in which :math:`q` is linear, chosen by structure so that neither
the chart nor the name of the branch depends on the names of the parameters. The chart is chained
with the section of the continuous symmetries; it is ``injective`` where the coefficient of
:math:`x` in :math:`q` has a sign certificate, and it ``reach``-es the whole side where :math:`x(\delta,
\theta')` has a positivity certificate; a claim without certificate is listed under ``unproven``. A
fiber of more than two points has no branch charts.
